About these numbers
GDP
IMF World Economic Outlook. The ten largest economies, the PPP ranking and growth rates are from the October 2026 edition; ranks 11 to 44 are from April 2026, as the October table for them wasn't retrieved. Hover a bar to see which edition.
Trade
WTO merchandise (goods) trade in US dollars for 2025, which counts intra-EU trade and re-exports, so hubs like the Netherlands and Hong Kong rank high. Services trade (finance, software, tourism) isn't in these rankings. Volume forecasts are from the WTO's 8 October 2026 outlook.
Critical minerals
IEA Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026, as reported by the Bank of Finland's BOFIT. Rare earths are shown for both mining and magnet-metal refining.
Hormuz
US EIA Short-Term Energy Outlook, August 2026.
Country profiles and map colours
Compiled by Claude from general knowledge of national trade profiles (OEC, UN Comtrade, USGS), not looked up product by product. They describe long-standing strengths, not exact current rankings.
Services
WTO commercial services exports for 2025 (March 2026 report), counting intra-EU trade. India's figure is the Government of India's fiscal 2025–26 total, as the WTO's 2025 value for India wasn't retrieved.
Who sells to whom
China's flows are from Chinese customs; flows into the US are from US import records; flows into Mexico are from Mexican data via UN Comtrade; US exports to China are from the US Census Bureau. Importers and exporters record the same trade differently (shipping costs, re-exports via hubs), so China's own figure for sales to the US is higher than the US figure for purchases from China.
Trade matrix
Each buyer's own 2025 import figures by partner, from UN Comtrade as published by Trading Economics. Only each buyer's top 20 to 25 partners are published, so smaller flows show as a dot. Because every cell comes from the buyer's records, the two directions of a pair come from different countries' customs.
Services between countries
US Bureau of Economic Analysis figures for 2024, as compiled in AmCham EU's Transatlantic Economy 2026. Comparable country-by-country services data for other economies isn't published in one place.
Tariffs
US average rate: Yale Budget Lab's State of US Tariffs reports, pre-substitution. Partner rates: USTR's Section 301 final action of 23 July 2026, as summarised by trade law firms. Sector tariffs: Yale Budget Lab and US proclamations. Other countries' averages: WTO Tariff and Trade Data. Antidumping duties, product exemptions and other countries' retaliation are not shown.
Region filter
Narrows the map, GDP, growth, trade, services, corridor, matrix, tariff and profile views to one region. Headline figures, critical minerals, Hormuz, US tariff history and sector tariffs are global and stay as they are. A corridor or matrix cell stays highlighted when either end is in the region; blocs count as Europe (EU) and Southeast Asia (ASEAN).
Left out
Bilateral flows beyond the 12 matrix economies, services flows that don't involve the US, tariffs charged by countries other than the US on specific partners, and countries not profiled (shown grey on the map).

Technology

AI and the technology frontier

Companies are spending at a record pace on AI, yet adoption differs sharply by industry and most firms have not turned it into profit. This view shows where AI is in use, how it is changing each industry, which technologies are advancing worldwide, and what that means for supply chains. Technology data compiled 10 November 2026 from the Stanford AI Index 2026, McKinsey State of AI 2026, US Census Bureau, Statistics Canada, IEA, IFR, WSTS, BloombergNEF and company reports.

Adoption

Who is using AI, and who is profiting from it

About one in four US businesses now uses AI, but the range runs from 45% in information to 12.6% in transportation and warehousing. Use is close to universal among large companies surveyed by McKinsey, while profit from it is not.
